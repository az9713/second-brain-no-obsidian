## Summary

Benedict Evans argues coding is AI's first undeniable product-market-fit use case: developers integrate models into an existing digital workflow, verify outputs, iterate quickly, and pull the product out of vendors' hands. That does not settle the broader market structure — whether foundation models commoditize, whether value accrues to labs or applications, whether chatbots are the wrong interface, what a more competitive, easier-to-build software market (Evans expects more competition, not more consolidation, though the resulting margin/pricing structure is unclear) does to software, and whether the capex boom can earn its return. Evans's stance is analytical humility: AI is transformative, but we are early enough that the important questions are the ones without clear answers.

## Source-Backed Claims

- Coding is the clearest breakout because developers are technical users, the work is digital, outputs can be tested, and agentic coding improved dramatically.
- OpenAI and Anthropic show divergent strategies: OpenAI explored many surfaces (asking "what else can we build"); Anthropic, with less capital raised, focused on coding and got it working.
- Foundation models may have weak durable differentiation if several firms produce comparable models and customers abstract them behind applications.
- Chatbots may not be the final interface; durable software may be horizontal/vertical tools, workflow systems, skills, agents, or app-specific layers.
- Model labs cannot build every useful application, just as Microsoft and Apple did not build every Windows or iPhone app.
- AI may produce more software, not less, by making previously too-niche or too-expensive workflows automatable.
- AI-native software may split between probabilistic reasoning layers and deterministic systems of record that cannot tolerate mistakes.
- Many valuable enterprise workflows are undocumented, implicit, political, or exception-heavy, making them harder to automate than a clean demo suggests.
- AI infrastructure spend is massive but constrained by financial gravity; ROI may be hard to measure because gains can become consumer surplus or competitive necessity rather than visible new revenue.
