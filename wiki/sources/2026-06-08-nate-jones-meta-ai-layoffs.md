## Summary

Nate B. Jones argues "AI layoffs" are not one phenomenon. The same headline can mean capital reallocation, founder-led organizational redesign, activity-based performance cuts, narrative-building without numbers, or ordinary business distress dressed up as AI. His warning: read layoffs as strategy signals, not as proof AI is replacing workers. Meta is framed as a hyperscaler capex tradeoff (money redirected to GPUs/datacenters/talent, not work already automated); Block/Dorsey is framed as closer to a real AI-native redesign but still incomplete without operational detail and metrics.

## Source-Backed Claims

- Five layoff categories: (1) capex reallocation, (2) visionary AI-native redesign, (3) activity-based performance cuts, (4) "hope layoffs" (AI invoked without metrics), (5) layoffs largely unrelated to AI (overhiring, margin pressure, weak demand, debt, management failure).
- Meta-style cuts can be about funding the AI capex race rather than AI productivity already replacing thousands of workers.
- A credible AI-native layoff should come with a visible operating thesis: which workflows changed, which roles changed, which metrics improved, what the company does differently.
- Block is treated as a more substantive example because Dorsey connects cuts to how the company should operate, not only to cost reduction.
- Companies may use AI language to make ordinary cost-cutting sound strategic.
- Job seekers should prefer companies with real AI-adoption signals over companies using AI as vague justification for shrinking.
