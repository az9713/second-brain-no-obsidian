## Summary

Nikesh Arora argues AI is already changing cybersecurity, enterprise software, and operations concretely. The headline claim: an Anthropic security capability ("Mythos"-like) found vulnerabilities in Palo Alto's own codebase in six weeks that would normally take five to seven years to find. The broader thesis is that AI democratizes intelligence inside companies — making customer-facing staff more consistent, compressing vulnerability discovery, weakening standalone analytical SaaS, and pushing value toward application workflows, memory, harnesses, and security telemetry. The strategic warning: the same capability helps attackers, raising systemic risk for small/mid-sized orgs, legacy software, OT, open source, and slow-patching vendors.

## Source-Backed Claims

- Arora says Palo Alto found vulnerabilities in six weeks that would normally take five to seven years to find.
- Advanced AI can perform persistent vulnerability search and chain issues into new attack paths (useful for defenders and attackers).
- He expects Mythos-level capabilities to become broadly available soon, including through open or foreign models.
- Enterprises need far more memory, context, and cyber telemetry to know normal vs. abnormal behavior in an AI-attacker world.
- "Analytical SaaS is over" in the medium term because models can analyze enterprise data directly; infrastructure software and systems of record/work remain valuable but get re-engineered around agents.
- Enterprise UI is likely to shrink as agents perform work directly through backend systems.
- Models may become a utility layer, with applications and workflow-specific systems capturing more profit.
- False positives are critical: Arora cites a ~30% false-positive rate in the security-model test and argues enterprise use needs far tighter control.
- AI may increase technical hiring at Palo Alto because every product and workflow needs transformation.
